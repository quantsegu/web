03 / Permissions

One truck. Several local decisions.

Start with the authority responsible for your business base, then disclose every fixed pitch and event. Do not assume one location’s approval covers a route through four cantons.

ZH / ZURICH

Food registration + site permission

Register the food business before opening. The canton identifies potential hospitality, itinerant-trade and public-space permits depending on the operation. There are exceptions for certain alcohol-free businesses with at most ten standing or seated places, and for itinerant trading at officially designated markets. Have the municipality classify your exact setup.

Canton’s foodtruck guide ↗
ZG / ZUG

Food control + municipal classification

Use the cantonal food-control business registration route. Ask the relevant municipality about hospitality, alcohol and use of public ground for your menu and service format. A private-site rental does not itself settle hospitality permissions.

Food-control registration ↗ · Municipal permit example ↗
LU / LUCERNE

Takeaway format matters

The cantonal factsheet distinguishes pure mobile takeaway sales from food intended for immediate consumption on-site or nearby, or with standing/seated places; the latter can require a hospitality permit. The pure-sales guidance excludes alcohol. Send an operating concept to the police and contact food control; the municipality or owner controls the pitch.

Police: ggp@lu.ch / 041 248 84 84. Lucerne city public space: 041 208 78 02.

Cantonal foodtruck factsheet ↗ · City application portal ↗
AG / AARGAU

Register the operator and fixed pitch

The March 2026 foodtruck guidance assigns food-control responsibility according to the business location; a fixed pitch must also be reported. It requires documented self-checks, suitable hygiene, cold-chain control and staff toilet access. Public pitches are handled by municipalities, private pitches by owners. The event guidance specifies notification at least ten days before a qualifying individual event.

Foodtruck factsheet ↗ · Registration / event notification ↗
Before signing a site contract: send the municipality your address, truck dimensions including awning, trading days, menu, preparation location, power load, water/wastewater plan, seating and alcohol plans. Ask whether planning/building, fire/gas, hospitality or itinerant-trade checks apply. This guide summarises the linked guidance; your operation still needs an individual determination.
04 / Next steps

Turn a shortlist into a launch plan

Define the offer

Choose the menu, target selling price, service speed, preparation base and whether you will offer alcohol or seating.

Request real availability

Use the listed offers for direct enquiries. For existing stops, ask owners about complementary days. Save your preferred locations above.

Get a written cost breakdown

Specify frontage and total footprint. Ask for rent, utilities, tax, deposit and commissions on the same quote.

Clear the operating requirements

Register the business with food control and obtain the relevant site and trading approvals before opening.

Match the vehicle to the site

Check towing or driving licence needs, payload, kitchen layout, electrical load, water tanks, waste handling and site access.

Run a measured trial

Track sales, waste, service time and daily contribution. Use your real results to choose a recurring route and larger events.

Complete go-live activity list

Every activity between a signed pitch and your first service, in a workable order.

1. Lock the concept

Menu, cuisine, price point, service speed, prep-heavy vs. cook-to-order, alcohol/seating, and your target customer.

2. Set up the business

Choose a legal form, register with AHV/social insurance, open a business bank account, decide VAT registration (mandatory above CHF 100k turnover), set up bookkeeping.

3. Buy or lease the vehicle

Trailer vs. self-propelled truck vs. rental to start. Check payload, kitchen layout, water tanks, waste handling and electrical load against your menu.

4. Register and insure the vehicle

Cantonal registration and plates, roadworthiness inspection (MFK), confirm your licence covers the vehicle's weight (C1 above 3.5t), vehicle insurance, product-liability insurance, business-interruption cover.

5. Register the food business

Notify the cantonal food-control authority, appoint a responsible person, prepare a self-check/HACCP-style hygiene concept, book the first inspection.

6. Clear site-specific permissions

Hospitality permit only if seating/on-site consumption applies; itinerant-trade permit or exception; alcohol licensing if relevant; fire/gas safety sign-off if cooking with LPG.

7. Secure your pitches

Request written offers, negotiate rent/commission/deposit, get a full cost breakdown before signing — use the shortlist and enquiry drafts above.

8. Build the supply chain

Ingredient and packaging suppliers, gas/fuel, and a licensed disposal route for grease and food waste.

9. Set up payment and back office

POS/card terminal, pricing that covers your break-even (use the calculator above), a simple system for tracking daily sales and costs.

10. Staff and train

Hire if needed, register staff for social insurance, ensure everyone handling food has hygiene training appropriate to the canton.

11. Brand and go to market

Name, truck livery, menu boards, social media presence, a simple website or listing so customers can find your schedule.

12. Run a measured trial

One recurring weekday pitch plus one market appearance; track meals sold, waste and service time before committing to a longer term.

13. Stay on top of renewals

Annual vehicle tax, food-control inspections, insurance renewals, and any time-limited pitch or market permits.
