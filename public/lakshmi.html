Alongside that I co-founded TraceGov.ai (EU AI governance) and Lekhly (financial document infrastructure).

At work: a medallion lakehouse with quality gates and CI/CD across five environments, a 74-entity conceptual data model and a 194-term business glossary with lineage in an OpenMetadata catalogue.

In the open: HamiltonVault, a Data Vault engine with 100 passing tests, validated row by row against AutomateDV, loading 200k rows in about two seconds on DuckDB. Also HamiltonFlow, SQLMeshVault and SQLMeshFlow on GitHub.

Earlier: a platform to replace SAS and an Oracle warehouse at APG, and a grid that cut a risk run from about 18 hours to 40 minutes at HSBC.

With statistics. In 2005 I was a research assistant at the Bombay Stock Exchange, using R and SPSS to find patterns in exchange data and writing weekly reports.

From there: trade finance at Bank of America, quant support on the UBS trading floor in Hong Kong, then HSBC, where I moved from reporting to portfolio systems to heading innovation. The trading-floor years taught me that good data decides whether a model, or a trader, can be trusted.

I am techno-functional: never only the business analyst and never only the engineer. I sit with the people who use the data, pin down the real requirement, then design something that works across platforms.

I have led teams of 22 (as architect at Sinewave), 14 data scientists (Rabobank) and 6 architects (APG), where I also chaired the Architecture Review Board and Design Authority. I work with regulators, external consultancies and open-source communities, including FINOS.

BCBS 239 data aggregation and lineage, DORA, SFTR, MiFID II and EMIR reporting, OFAC screening rules from my trade-finance years, and the EU's AI governance direction through TraceGov.ai.

At Rabobank I worked directly with the European Central Bank and the Dutch central bank on model reviews and data presentations.
