Herbalist's
Notes

Eating for thyroid support

  • Selenium & zinc help convert T4 into active T3 — Brazil nuts, pumpkin seeds, and hemp seeds do this work below.
  • Iron deficiency is common alongside hypothyroidism — always paired here with vitamin C for absorption.
  • timingIf you're on thyroid medication: take it on an empty stomach, then wait 30–60+ minutes before coffee, dairy, calcium, or iron. Treat these drinks as a proper breakfast well after your dose.
  • Iodine — don't self-add kelp or seaweed. Ordinary iodized salt is enough; excess iodine can flare autoimmune thyroid conditions.

Kitchen
Notes

How this week works — revised for 2,200 kcal / high protein

  • Updated target: ~2,200 kcal/day, ~165–175g protein. To fit real protein into a tighter calorie budget, every smoothie now includes a scoop of protein powder, dal/paneer portions are bigger, and oil/ghee/cream/nuts are trimmed back — protein-dense foods carry more nutrition per calorie than added fats do.
  • Rice is now ~120g cooked (not 150g) and most dinners use 2 chapati instead of 3 — the calories saved get redirected into dal and paneer, not cut outright.
  • Evening snacks shifted from nuts/toast (calorie-dense) to buttermilk, curd, or roasted chana (protein-dense, lighter).
  • Have your morning drink after your medication window, as a late breakfast.
  • Cabbage and cauliflower appear once each, always cooked, never raw or daily.
  • These numbers are estimates from recipe composition, not lab-measured — for a target this precise, weighing portions and logging in an app (Cronometer, MyFitnessPal) for a week will tell you exactly where you land.
  • Mozzarella works too — 30g fresh mozzarella diced into the cucumber-tomato salad adds ~6g protein for ~70 kcal, a good neutral swap when you want a change from paneer.

Full Recipes
Dals
Moong Dal

1 cup yellow moong dal · 1 tomato · ½ tsp turmeric · 1 tsp cumin seeds · 1 clove garlic · 1 tsp ghee · salt · coriander

Boil dal with turmeric and salt until soft, 15–20 min. Temper cumin and garlic in ghee, pour over the dal, simmer with chopped tomato 5 min. Garnish with coriander.

Chana Dal

1 cup chana dal (soaked 30 min) · 1 onion · 1 tomato · ginger-garlic paste · turmeric · coriander powder · oil · salt

Boil dal 20–25 min until soft but holding shape. Sauté onion until golden, add ginger-garlic, tomato, and spices, cook to a paste. Add dal, simmer 10 min.

Toor Dal

1 cup toor dal · 1 tomato · curry leaves · mustard seeds · 1 dried red chili · pinch asafoetida · turmeric · salt

Pressure cook dal with turmeric and salt until mushy. Temper mustard seeds, curry leaves, chili, and asafoetida in oil, pour over the dal, simmer 5 min.

Masoor Dal

1 cup red masoor dal · 1 onion · 1 tomato · 1 tsp cumin · turmeric · garlic · salt

Cooks fast — boil dal with turmeric 15 min until soft. Sauté onion, garlic, cumin, and tomato, stir into the dal, simmer 5–8 min.

Dal Makhani (Quick Version)

½ cup whole urad dal (soaked overnight) · ¼ cup cooked rajma · 1 tbsp butter · 2 tbsp cream or milk · 1 tomato pureed · ginger-garlic · garam masala · salt

Pressure cook the soaked urad dal 20 min until soft, stir in cooked rajma. Sauté ginger-garlic in butter, add tomato puree and garam masala, cook down, mix into the dal, finish with cream, simmer 10 min.

Moong Dal Soup / Light Khichdi

½ cup split moong dal · ¼ cup rice (for khichdi) · cumin · turmeric · ghee · ginger · salt

Boil dal (and rice, if making khichdi) with turmeric, ginger, and salt until soft and porridge-like. Temper with ghee and cumin. Easy on the stomach for a Sunday night.

Legume Curries
Rajma (Kidney Bean Curry)

1 cup rajma (soaked overnight, boiled soft) · 1 onion · 2 tomatoes · ginger-garlic · cumin · coriander powder · garam masala · oil · salt

Sauté onion until golden, add ginger-garlic, spices, and tomatoes, cook to a thick gravy. Add the boiled rajma with a little cooking liquid, simmer 15–20 min until thick.

Chana Masala / Chole

1 cup chickpeas (soaked, boiled soft) · 1 onion · 2 tomatoes · ginger-garlic · cumin · coriander powder · garam masala · pinch amchur (optional) · oil · salt

Same method as rajma — build an onion-tomato-spice gravy, add chickpeas, simmer 15 min. Add amchur for the tangier "chole" version (Wednesday); skip it for the milder "chana masala" (Saturday).

Vegetable Sabzis
Bhindi (Okra) Sabzi

250 g okra, sliced · 1 onion · turmeric · coriander powder · oil · salt

Sauté okra alone on medium-high heat 8–10 min until it stops being sticky. Add onion, turmeric, coriander powder, and salt, cook another 8–10 min until tender.

Lauki (Bottle Gourd) Sabzi

1 medium lauki, peeled and cubed · cumin seeds · turmeric · 1 green chili · oil · salt

Temper cumin and chili in oil, add lauki, turmeric, and salt, cover and cook 12–15 min until soft, stirring occasionally.

Cooked Cabbage Sabzi

½ small cabbage, shredded · mustard seeds · pinch turmeric · 1 dried red chili · oil · salt

Temper mustard seeds and chili in oil, add cabbage and turmeric, stir-fry on medium heat 8–10 min until just tender — don't overcook past soft-crisp.

Mixed Vegetable Curry

2 cups mixed vegetables (carrot, beans, peas, potato) · 1 onion · 1 tomato · ginger-garlic · coriander powder · turmeric · oil · salt

Build an onion-tomato-spice base, add vegetables and a splash of water, cover and simmer 15–18 min until tender.

Vegetable Kurma

2 cups mixed vegetables · 2 tbsp grated coconut · fennel seeds · 1 green chili · 1 onion · ginger-garlic · oil · salt

Grind coconut, fennel, and green chili to a paste. Sauté onion and ginger-garlic, add vegetables and a little water, cook until nearly tender, stir in the coconut paste, simmer 5–8 min.

Sautéed Spinach

200 g spinach · 1 clove garlic, sliced · cumin seeds · oil · salt · lemon juice

Sauté garlic and cumin in oil until fragrant, add spinach, cook 3–4 min just until wilted, finish with lemon juice.

Paneer Dishes
Paneer Bhurji

200 g paneer, crumbled · 1 onion · 1 tomato · turmeric · 1 green chili · oil · salt · coriander

Sauté onion and chili until soft, add tomato and turmeric, cook down, fold in crumbled paneer, warm through 3–4 min. Garnish with coriander.

Paneer Tikka (Pan-Seared)

200 g paneer, cubed · 3 tbsp thick yogurt · ginger-garlic paste · tikka masala · turmeric · oil

Marinate paneer in yogurt and spices 20–30 min. Pan-sear in a hot skillet, turning until lightly charred on all sides, 6–8 min.

Palak Paneer

200 g spinach, blanched and pureed · 150 g paneer, cubed · 1 onion · ginger-garlic · turmeric · 1 tbsp cream · oil · salt

Sauté onion and ginger-garlic, add spinach puree and turmeric, simmer 5 min. Add paneer and cream, simmer 5 more min — don't overcook the paneer.

Paneer Curry (Butter-Masala Style)

200 g paneer, cubed · 1 onion · 2 tomatoes · ginger-garlic · garam masala · 1 tbsp butter · 2 tbsp cream · salt

Cook onion, ginger-garlic, and tomatoes to a soft base (blend smooth if you like it velvety). Return to the pan with butter and garam masala, add paneer and cream, simmer 5–8 min.

Salads, Snacks & Sides
Cucumber-Tomato Salad

1 cucumber · 1 tomato · lemon juice · salt · black pepper · coriander

Dice everything, toss with lemon juice, salt, and pepper. No cooking needed.

Sprouts Salad

1 cup mixed sprouts · 1 tomato · ½ onion · lemon juice · chaat masala · coriander

Lightly steam sprouts 3–4 min if you prefer them less raw, toss with diced vegetables, lemon juice, and chaat masala.

Roasted Chana Snack

1 cup boiled chickpeas · chaat masala · cumin powder · oil · salt

Pat chickpeas dry, toss with a little oil and spices, roast in a pan or oven at 180°C until crisp, 15–20 min.

Raita

1 cup curd · ½ cucumber, grated · roasted cumin powder · salt · coriander

Whisk curd smooth, fold in grated cucumber, season with roasted cumin and salt.

Mozzarella Caprese-Style Salad

30g fresh mozzarella, diced · 1 tomato · a few basil leaves · olive oil · black pepper · pinch salt

Layer or toss diced tomato and mozzarella with torn basil, a drizzle of olive oil, salt, and pepper. Good swap into the Cucumber-Tomato Salad or as its own light evening protein bite. ~6g protein, ~70 kcal for 30g mozzarella.

Coach's
Notes

Strength for longevity — not just aesthetics

  • Four days · ~60 minutes each. Three lifting days plus one full-body conditioning day. Rest or walk on the other three — that walking is part of the fat-loss plan, not optional filler.
  • Equipment only: your four dumbbells (light → heavy) and resistance bands. No barbell, no machines. Anchor bands to a sturdy door or post for rows and pulldowns.
  • Longevity priorities: hinge and squat patterns (legs + back health), carries (grip + spine stability), rows and face pulls (posture), single-leg work (balance as you age), and controlled tempo — no bouncing reps.
  • Recomposition at 43: train hard enough to keep muscle while the ~2,200 kcal plan handles fat loss. If gym performance drops for two weeks straight, add 100–150 kcal on training days — don't grind through exhaustion.
  • Thyroid note: moderate training is fine. Avoid stacking three all-out HIIT sessions on top of four lifting days — the full-body day uses brisk circuits, not gasping sprints.
  • Progression: when you hit the top of the rep range with good form on all sets, move up one dumbbell weight next session. Bands: use a thicker band or choke up to shorten the loop.

Every session — warm-up & cool-down
Warm-up · 8 minutes (never skip)

2 min marching in place or easy step-ups · 10 arm circles each direction · 10 bodyweight squats · 10 hip hinges (hands on thighs) · 10 band pull-aparts · 1 light set of the first exercise at 50% weight.

Cool-down · 5 minutes

Hamstring stretch 30 sec each leg · hip flexor stretch 30 sec each · chest doorway stretch 30 sec · thoracic rotation 8 each side · 5 deep belly breaths. On Day D the walk replaces most of this.
