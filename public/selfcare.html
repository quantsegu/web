The Kitchen Almanac

Five drinks, a week of high-protein meals, groceries, recipes, nutrition targets, your training plan and weight tracker, plus an outfit colour guide.

Recipes, day by day

Each day shows its meals with the full recipes underneath. A recipe is written out the first time it appears; later days point back to it. Every recipe shows its total time, including unattended time like pressure-cooker release. Weekday recipes take 30 minutes or less and weekend recipes 60 minutes or less. Lunch and dinner are each one session: start the dal in the cooker first and cook the sabzi while it runs. Soak the chickpeas and rajma on Friday night and boil them in the Saturday batch, so no weekday recipe needs a soak or a long boil.

Green Power Protein 5 min

Everyday driver. Iron, magnesium, calcium in one glass.

PROTEIN ~33g
  • Fresh spinach, packed30 g
  • Frozen banana1
  • Skyr or quark200 g
  • Peanut or almond butter20 g
  • Chia seeds15 g
  • Milk150 ml
  • Ground flaxseed10 g

Blend everything 45–60 sec until smooth. Let sit 3–5 min so the chia thickens.

IronCalciumB12Omega-3
Contains dairy + iron — keep 30–60 min clear of thyroid medication.
Lunch
Moong Dal 20 min

1 cup yellow moong dal · 1 tomato · ½ tsp turmeric · 1 tsp cumin seeds · 1 clove garlic · 1 tsp ghee · salt · coriander

Rinse the dal and pressure cook with turmeric and salt, 2 whistles, then let the pressure drop 3 min. Meanwhile temper cumin and garlic in ghee, cook the chopped tomato in it 3 min, pour in the dal and simmer 3 min. Garnish with coriander.

Bhindi (Okra) Sabzi 25 min

250 g okra, sliced · 1 onion · turmeric · coriander powder · oil · salt

Sauté okra alone on medium-high heat 8–10 min until it stops being sticky. Add onion, turmeric, coriander powder, and salt, cook another 8–10 min until tender.

Evening
Roasted Chana Snack 22 min

80 g dry chickpeas, boiled in the Saturday batch (about 200 g boiled) · chaat masala · cumin powder · oil · salt

Pat chickpeas dry, toss with a little oil and spices, roast in a pan or oven at 180°C until crisp, 15–20 min.

Dinner
Rajma (Kidney Bean Curry) 25 min

80 g dry rajma, boiled in the Saturday batch (about 200 g boiled) · 1 onion · 2 tomatoes · ginger-garlic · cumin · coriander powder · garam masala · oil · salt

Sauté onion 5 min until golden, add ginger-garlic and spices for 1 min, then tomatoes for 5 min to a thick gravy. Add the boiled rajma with a splash of its cooking water, mash a few beans against the pan and simmer 10 min.

Sautéed Spinach 8 min

200 g spinach · 1 clove garlic, sliced · cumin seeds · oil · salt · lemon juice

Sauté garlic and cumin in oil until fragrant, add spinach, cook 3–4 min just until wilted, finish with lemon juice.

Moong Chilka Dal 25 min

200 g split green moong dal with skin (moong chilka) · 1 onion · 1 tomato · ginger-garlic paste · turmeric · coriander powder · oil · salt

Rinse the dal and pressure cook with turmeric and salt, 3 whistles, then let the pressure drop 5 min. Meanwhile sauté onion 4 min, add ginger-garlic, spices and tomato and cook to a paste, 4 min. Stir in the dal and simmer 3 min.

Weekend Batch: Chickpeas and Rajma 55 min

320 g dry chickpeas · 200 g dry rajma (soaked overnight in separate bowls) · salt

Drain the soaked beans. Pressure cook each separately in fresh water with a pinch of salt: chickpeas 3 whistles, rajma 4 whistles, each followed by 5 min for the pressure to drop. That is about 25 min a round and mostly unattended, so roughly 55 min with one cooker or 30 min with two. Cool and divide into portions of about 200 g boiled (80 g dry), and keep their cooking water. Fridge what you use by Wednesday and freeze the rest, thawing it overnight. These portions feed the roasted chana, rajma, chole, both chickpea salads and the dal makhani.

Dal Makhani (Quick Version) 45 min

½ cup whole urad dal (soaked overnight) · 20 g dry rajma, boiled in the Saturday batch · 1 tbsp butter · 2 tbsp cream or milk · 1 tomato pureed · ginger-garlic · garam masala · salt

Soak the urad dal on Saturday night, then pressure cook it 20 min until soft and stir in the boiled rajma. Sauté ginger-garlic in butter, add tomato puree and garam masala, cook down, mix into the dal, finish with cream, simmer 10 min.

½ cup chana or toor dal · 1 cup raw banana, yam or zucchini · turmeric · grated coconut · cumin

Pressure cook the dal with turmeric, 3 whistles, then 5 min for the pressure to drop. Meanwhile cook the small-cut vegetable until tender, combine with the dal, then stir in coconut and cumin. A side for any lunch.

Paruppu Sadam 25 min

½ cup toor dal · 40 g raw rice · turmeric · 1 tsp ghee · salt

Pressure cook dal and rice together, 3 whistles plus 5 min for the pressure to drop, until very soft, then mash lightly, and stir in turmeric, salt and ghee. Easy lunch base.

1 tomato · ½ cup water saved from boiling toor dal · tamarind · pepper · cumin · garlic · salt

Soya
swaps

Paneer, soya paneer or soya chunks

  • Swap by protein, not by weight. Assumed per 100 g: paneer about 19 g protein and 290 kcal; soya paneer (firm tofu) about 14 g protein and 140 kcal; soya chunks, dry weight, about 52 g protein and 340 kcal. Brands differ a lot, so check the label of what you buy and adjust the grams.
  • Weigh soya chunks dry. Boil them 5 min in salted water, drain and squeeze out the water before using. Press tofu for 10 min first so it browns instead of steaming.
  • Both swaps are lighter. Per 100 g of paneer replaced, tofu saves about 100 kcal and soya chunks about 165 kcal. To stay near 2,200 kcal, add 1 tbsp oil or ghee (about 120 kcal) for each 100–150 g of paneer you swap.
  • Thyroid medication: soya can interfere with how thyroid medication is absorbed. Keep soya dishes well away from your dose, and ask your doctor or pharmacist how long a gap you need.

Notes

Eating for thyroid support

  • Selenium & zinc help convert T4 into active T3 — Brazil nuts, pumpkin seeds, and hemp seeds do this work below.
  • Iron deficiency is common alongside hypothyroidism — always paired here with vitamin C for absorption.
  • timingIf you're on thyroid medication: take it on an empty stomach, then wait 30–60+ minutes before dairy, calcium, or iron. Treat these drinks as a proper breakfast well after your dose.
  • Iodine — don't self-add kelp or seaweed. Ordinary iodized salt is enough; excess iodine can flare autoimmune thyroid conditions.

Notes

How this week works — revised for 2,200 kcal / high protein

  • Updated target: ~2,200 kcal/day, ~165–175g protein. To fit real protein into a tighter calorie budget, every smoothie now includes a scoop of protein powder, dal/paneer portions are bigger, and oil/ghee/cream/nuts are trimmed back — protein-dense foods carry more nutrition per calorie than added fats do.
  • All ingredient weights are raw, weighed dry before cooking. Rice roughly triples once cooked, so 40 g raw at lunch and 50 g raw at dinner come to about 120 g and 150 g on the plate. Dal and beans are weighed dry too. Dish servings in cups (for example "1.5 cup") are the finished dish as served. There is no chapati or bread — the calories saved elsewhere are redirected into dal and paneer, not cut outright.
  • Time rule: weekday recipes are 30 minutes or less, weekend recipes 60 minutes or less, and each recipe shows its time. Chickpeas and rajma are boiled once in the Saturday batch (soak them Friday night), which is what keeps the weekday bean dishes quick.
  • Saturday lunch is now soya chunk masala instead of chana masala, which makes Saturday about 2,080 kcal and 188 g protein. Add a spoon of ghee or a handful of peanuts if you want the calories back. Tuesday's chana dal became moong chilka dal because chana dal needs a long boil.
  • Evening snacks shifted from nuts (calorie-dense) to buttermilk, curd, or roasted chana (protein-dense, lighter).
  • Have your morning drink after your medication window, as a late breakfast.
  • Cabbage and cauliflower appear once each, always cooked, never raw or daily.
  • These numbers are estimates from recipe composition, not lab-measured — for a target this precise, weighing portions and logging in an app (Cronometer, MyFitnessPal) for a week will tell you exactly where you land.
  • Mozzarella works too — 30g fresh mozzarella diced into the cucumber-tomato salad adds ~6g protein for ~70 kcal, a good neutral swap when you want a change from paneer.
